# 🚀 Salesforce Sprint 4 – Building Business Logic with Apex

## About This Sprint

Sprint 4 marked an important step in my Salesforce learning journey because it introduced me to **Apex** and the concept of **business logic**. In the previous sprints, I focused on creating objects, fields, relationships, and designing the data model. During this sprint, I learned that storing data alone is not enough—an application must also make intelligent decisions based on business requirements.

I understood how real-world Salesforce applications use business rules to validate user actions, prevent incorrect data from being saved, and automate decision-making. This sprint also taught me that before writing any code, it is important to understand the business problem and design a proper solution.

## Key Takeaways

One of the biggest lessons I learned during this sprint is that **good software is designed before it is developed**. Instead of directly writing code, I learned to first understand the business requirements, identify the business rules, and then decide how those rules should be implemented.

I also understood why each class should have a single responsibility. Separating business logic into different service classes makes the application easier to understand, maintain, and extend in the future. Using meaningful class names and method names also improves code readability and helps other developers understand the project more easily.

Another valuable lesson was learning that Apex is not just another programming language—it is a way of implementing business decisions inside Salesforce applications.
